# **Computational Poetics: A Theoretical and Practical Framework for Transmuting Ideation Transcripts into High-Literary Verse**

## **1\. Introduction: The Divergence of Algorithmic Probability and Literary Aesthetics**

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into creative workflows has fundamentally altered the landscape of ideation and content generation. As users increasingly rely on systems like Claude Code to serve as intellectual sparring partners—engaging in "podcast-style" interviews to generate deep, rich histories of thought—the challenge shifts from information retrieval to aesthetic transmutation. The user’s specific requirement—to convert these rich, prose-heavy transcripts into "great poems," ranging from lyrical prose to epics, without the pitfalls of archaic diction, forced rhyme, or structural disintegration—highlights a critical friction point in current generative AI capabilities.

The core of this problem lies in the ontological difference between how a probabilistic model conceives of "poetry" and how literary theory defines it. When an unoptimized AI is prompted to write a poem, it relies on statistical patterns found in its training data. Unfortunately, the most statistically dominant forms of poetry in the public domain—and thus in the model’s weights—are often nursery rhymes, greeting card verse, and 18th or 19th-century public domain literature. This results in what literary scholars might call "doggerel": verse characterized by forced AABB rhyme schemes, archaic vocabulary (e.g., "betwixt," "alas"), and a superficial treatment of the subject matter.1 The model "hallucinates" a poetic aesthetic that feels foreign to the modern ear because it is simulating a simulacrum of poetry rather than engaging with the craft elements that define high-quality verse: voice, diction, imagery, syntax, and organic form.3

To bridge the gap between the "weird words" of the current output and the "nuanced, lyrical" output desired, we must look beyond standard prompt engineering and deep into the academic frameworks of **Creative Writing**, **Literary Theory**, and **Qualitative Research Methodologies**. Specifically, we must operationalize concepts such as T.S. Eliot’s *Objective Correlative*, Denise Levertov’s *Organic Form*, and the social science method of *Poetic Inquiry* to build a rigorous "Claude Skill." This report provides an exhaustive analysis of these frameworks, creating a theoretical bridge between the "messy" data of human speech and the crystallized form of literary art. It argues that the solution is not to ask the AI to *generate* poetry from scratch, but to instruct it to *transmute* the existing transcript using the tools of the "Found Poetry" and "Verbatim Theatre" traditions.

**2\. The Anatomy of the Failure: Why AI Struggles with Contemporary Poetics**

To engineer a solution, one must first dissect the failure modes of the current system. The user reports three specific symptoms: "funny language/weird words," "overemphasis on rhyming," and "loss of structure." These are not random errors; they are predictable artifacts of how LLMs process language when constrained by the token "poem."

### **2.1 The "Archaic Diction" Hallucination and Style Transfer**

The complaint regarding "funny language" or "weird words" points to a misalignment in **Diction**—one of the foundational elements of poetry.3 Diction refers to the specific selection of words to achieve a sensory or intellectual effect. In academic literary analysis, diction is often categorized into a hierarchy: *High* (formal, lofty), *Middle* (neutral, correct), and *Low* (informal, conversational).4

When an LLM is prompted to "write a poem," it often performs an implicit style transfer, shifting from the *Middle Diction* of the user's transcript to a *High Diction* it associates with "Poetry" (capital P). The model’s training data includes vast repositories of Victorian and Romantic era poetry (e.g., Tennyson, Keats, Longfellow) because these texts are out of copyright. Consequently, the model statistically over-indexes on words that signal "poeticness" in that specific historical context—words like "dwell," "midst," "unfold," "tapestry," and "symphony."

For a modern user discussing a technical or personal topic in a "podcast-style" interview, the intrusion of these anachronisms creates a jarring dissonance. The "voice" of the poem—which should reflect the speaker's attitude and personality 3—is hijacked by the generic "voice" of a 19th-century bard. This rupture in voice destroys the authenticity of the output. The AI fails to realize that contemporary poetry heavily favors *Middle* and *Low* diction, prioritizing the "natural speech" and "conversational" rhythms of the speaker over artificial elevation.5

### **2.2 The "Rhyme Trap" and Semantic Drift**

The second failure mode—"overemphasizes rhyming"—is a structural trap. LLMs generate text token by token. When a model commits to a rhyming structure (especially the simple AABB or ABAB schemes prevalent in its training data), the constraint of the *sound* begins to dictate the choice of the *meaning*. This phenomenon, known in computational linguistics as "semantic drift," occurs when the model chooses a word not because it makes sense contextually, but because it satisfies the phonological requirement of the rhyme scheme.6

If the transcript discusses "data analysis," and the first line ends in "code," the model is statistically compelled to end the next line with "road," "load," or "mode," even if those words force the narrative into a nonsensical direction. This explains the user's observation that the language becomes "funny"; the semantic integrity of the rich transcript is being sacrificed on the altar of the rhyme. The model is prioritizing the *sound pattern* (the nursery rhyme effect) over the *informational fidelity* of the transcript.

### **2.3 Loss of Structure and the "Carriage Return" Effect**

The "loss of structure" refers to the model’s inability to understand **Lineation** (line breaks) as a function of meaning. In prose, the line break is determined by the page margin. In poetry, the line break is an active punctuation mark that controls rhythm, emphasis, and breath.7

Current LLMs often treat line breaks as mere formatting decorations—simply hitting "enter" after every comma or clause to make the text *look* like a poem visually, without understanding the *musical* or *syntactic* logic behind the break. This results in "choppy" verse that lacks a cohesive rhythm. The academic solution to this is found in the theories of **Organic Form**, specifically those proposed by Denise Levertov, which link the line break to the "breath" and "thought process" of the speaker—a perfect heuristic for converting spoken transcripts into verse.8

**3\. Academic Frameworks for Imagery and Objectivity**

To correct the AI's tendency toward abstraction and archaic diction, we must program the "Claude Skill" with specific literary frameworks that prioritize **Imagery** and **Objectivity**. The goal is to move the AI from "telling" (abstract summary) to "showing" (concrete imagery).

### **3.1 T.S. Eliot’s Objective Correlative**

The most potent theoretical tool for correcting AI abstraction is T.S. Eliot’s concept of the **Objective Correlative**. In his 1919 essay *Hamlet and His Problems*, Eliot argued that emotion in art cannot be expressed directly by naming the feeling (e.g., "I was sad"). Instead, the artist must find "a set of objects, a situation, a chain of events which shall be the formula of that particular emotion".9 When these external facts are presented, the emotion is immediately evoked in the reader without needing to be labeled.

This framework is crucial for processing "ideation transcripts." Ideation often involves abstract feelings—uncertainty about a project, the thrill of discovery, the weight of responsibility. An unoptimized AI will simply state these feelings: "The project brought us joy / We felt the excitement deploy." This is weak poetry.

By applying the *Objective Correlative*, we can instruct the AI to scan the transcript not for the *names* of emotions, but for the *objects* and *situations* present when those emotions occurred. If the transcript mentions "staying up until 4 AM drinking stale coffee" during a breakthrough, the *Objective Correlative* for the "excitement" is the "stale coffee at 4 AM." The poem should describe the coffee, not the excitement.

| Element | Standard AI Approach | Objective Correlative Approach |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Technique** | Direct Statement / Abstraction | Evocation through Objects |
| **Example** | "I felt lonely in the city." | "A pair of ragged claws scuttling across the floors of silent seas." (Eliot) |
| **Instruction** | "Summarize the feelings." | "Identify the physical objects present in the scene. Describe the objects to evoke the feeling." |

### **3.2 The Concrete vs. Abstract Spectrum**

Closely related to Eliot’s theory is the linguistic distinction between **Concrete** and **Abstract** diction. This distinction is a staple of MFA Creative Writing pedagogy and is essential for grounding AI output.4

* **Abstract Diction:** Refers to qualities, concepts, and ideas that are intangible. Examples include "freedom," "love," "efficiency," "success," "synergy." These words invite the reader to use *reason* but disconnect them from the *senses*.11  
* **Concrete Diction:** Refers to specific objects that can be perceived by the five senses. Examples include "velvet," "rust," "smoke," "apple," "clicking keyboard." These words trigger a sensory response in the reader.12

The user’s complaint about "weird words" often stems from the AI’s reliance on high-level abstractions which feel hollow. "Lyrical" writing is defined by its sensory richness. As Ren Powell notes, concrete objects bring the reader back to the body.11 Therefore, the "Claude Skill" must include a pre-processing step that acts as a "Concrete Filter." The AI must be instructed to extract concrete nouns from the transcript and prioritize them over abstract summaries. If the transcript is about "innovation" (abstract), the AI must hunt for the "whiteboard," the "prototype," or the "tangled wires" (concrete) mentioned in the interview.

### **3.3 Imagism and the "Direct Treatment of the Thing"**

The Imagist movement of the early 20th century, led by Ezra Pound and H.D., provides a rigorous rule set for modern poetry that opposes the "flowery" Victorian style the AI tends to imitate. The three principles of Imagism are:

1. Direct treatment of the "thing" whether subjective or objective.  
2. To use absolutely no word that does not contribute to the presentation.  
3. As regarding rhythm: to compose in the sequence of the musical phrase, not in the sequence of a metronome.12

These rules are directly applicable to the user’s problem.

* **Direct Treatment:** Solves the "weird words" issue by forcing the AI to focus on the subject matter of the transcript rather than "poetic" ornamentation.  
* **No Superfluous Words:** Solves the "funny language" issue by cutting adjectives like "glorious" or "mystical" which the AI adds for flavor.  
* **Musical Phrase:** Solves the "overemphasis on rhyming" and "loss of structure" by validating *Free Verse* and *Organic Rhythm* over the rigid "metronome" of AABB rhyme schemes.

**4\. Structural Frameworks: Narratives, Epics, and Lyrics**

The user explicitly asks for "epics," "lyrical prose," and "narrative poems" that do not rely on rhyme. To achieve this, we must provide the AI with structural templates that historically support these modes without the crutch of end-rhyme.

### **4.1 The Narrative Poem and the Modern Epic**

Narrative poetry is a form that tells a story, featuring characters, a plot, and a setting.14 Given that the source material is a "podcast-style" interview, the content is inherently narrative—it likely follows an arc of problem identification, struggle, and resolution.

The **Epic** is a sub-genre of narrative poetry traditionally characterized by length, serious subject matter, and "heroic" scope. While ancient epics (Homer) used strict meters, the **Modern Epic** (e.g., *The Cantos* by Ezra Pound, *Paterson* by William Carlos Williams, or recent works by Kae Tempest) often utilizes a collage-like structure that mixes high lyricism with mundane details.16

For the user’s "rich information" transcripts, the Modern Epic is the ideal container. It allows for:

* **The Inclusion of History:** Epics traditionally contain history. The transcript's "rich history of information" can be preserved in "Cantos" or chapters within the poem.  
* **The Catalog (Enumeration):** A classic epic device is the "Catalog" (e.g., the catalog of ships in the *Iliad*). The AI can be prompted to use "Whitmanesque lists" to cover the breadth of topics in the ideation session without needing to force them into a narrative cause-and-effect structure.18  
* **Variable Diction:** Modern epics allow for the shifting between "street level" (Low) and "mythic" (High) diction, mirroring the dynamic range of a deep conversation.

### **4.2 Blank Verse: The "High Dignity" Alternative to Rhyme**

If the user desires a poem that feels "classical" or "epic" but hates the "funny" rhymes, **Blank Verse** is the academic solution. Blank verse is unrhymed iambic pentameter.19

* **Mechanism:** The line consists of five iambs (da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM). This meter mimics the natural heartbeat and is the closest formal meter to natural English speech.  
* **Why it optimizes AI output:** It creates a "container" for the language that elevates it above prose (satisfying the "poetic" requirement) but removes the rhyme constraint (eliminating "weird words" and semantic drift). Milton chose Blank Verse for *Paradise Lost* specifically to avoid the "jingling sound of like endings," which he viewed as a constraint on serious expression.19  
* **Implementation:** The AI can be prompted to write in "Loose Blank Verse" or "Hendecasyllabics" (11-syllable lines) to give the poem a rolling, epic cadence without the forced jingle.

### **4.3 Free Verse and Denise Levertov’s "Organic Form"**

For the "lyrical" output that "loses its structure," we must turn to **Free Verse**. Free Verse is not the absence of form; it is the presence of *Organic Form*. Denise Levertov, a central figure in American postmodern poetry, argued that in organic poetry, "form is never more than a revelation of content".8

Levertov’s theory of **Lineation** is particularly useful for processing transcripts. She viewed the line break as a form of "scoring" the text for the voice, similar to musical notation.7

* **The Breath Unit:** The line should end where the breath pauses.  
* **The Thought Unit:** The line should break where the thought shifts.  
* **Enjambment:** Breaking a line in the middle of a sentence creates tension and propulsion.  
* **End-Stopping:** Ending a line with punctuation creates rest and finality.

By instructing the AI to treat the transcript as a "musical score" and to use line breaks to indicate the *pauses* and *accelerations* of the speaker's thought process, we restore the "structure" the user feels is lost. We replace the arbitrary visual structure of AI (chopped blocks) with the *sonic* structure of the speaker's voice.

### **4.4 Prose Poetry: The Lyrical Block**

The user specifically requests "Prose that's written in lyrical ways." This corresponds to the genre of the **Prose Poem**.20 A prose poem appears as a block of text (like a paragraph) but uses the devices of poetry: heightened imagery, rhythm, compression, and sound play (alliteration, assonance).18

* **Advantage:** This completely removes the "line break" issue where the AI might struggle. It allows the model to focus entirely on *Cadence* and *Imagery*.  
* **Optimization:** The AI should be prompted to write "Prose Poetry" using "parataxis" (short, punchy sentences) and "internal rhyme" rather than end-rhyme.

**5\. Methodologies of Transmutation: Turning Data into Art**

The user’s workflow—ideating with an AI, creating a transcript, and then generating poetry from that transcript—mirrors several established research and artistic methodologies. By explicitly invoking these methodologies in the prompt engineering, we can align the AI’s "mental model" with the user’s goal.

### **5.1 Poetic Inquiry: A Research Methodology for Ideation**

**Poetic Inquiry** is an arts-based qualitative research methodology used in the social sciences. Researchers collect data (often interview transcripts, just like the user's) and then "transmute" that data into poetry to analyze and present the findings.21

* **The Process:** The researcher acts as a "filter," reading the transcript to identify "luminescent data"—words or phrases that carry high emotional or theoretical weight. These phrases are extracted and arranged into a poem that captures the "essence" of the participant's experience.23  
* **Relevance to User:** This is exactly what the user wants: to turn the "rich information" of the ideation session into a "really great poem" that preserves the depth of the topic.  
* **Implementation:** The Claude Skill should be framed as a "Poetic Inquiry Researcher." The instructions should not be "Write a poem about X," but rather: "Perform a Poetic Inquiry analysis on this transcript. Identify the core themes and the most resonant phrases. Synthesize these findings into a poem that distills the transcript's insight." This shifts the task from *invention* (making things up) to *synthesis* (distilling the truth).

### **5.2 Verbatim Theatre and the "Poetry of Speech"**

**Verbatim Theatre**, pioneered by artists like **Anna Deavere Smith**, involves creating performance scripts derived word-for-word from interviews.24 Smith argues that people speak in poetry when they are passionate; the "ums," "ahs," and false starts (disfluencies) are part of the rhythm of their thought.24

* **The "Weird Words" Solution:** Verbatim Theatre offers a radical solution to the "weird words" problem. If the AI is constrained to use *only* the vocabulary found in the transcript (or slight variations thereof), it cannot physically output archaic words like "betwixt" or "alas" because the user did not say them.  
* **Capturing Voice:** By instructing the AI to use "Verbatim Theatre techniques," we encourage it to retain the unique syntax of the user’s speech—the specific way they phrase an idea—rather than smoothing it out into "diplomatic" or "neutral" AI prose.26 This results in "Lyrical Prose" that sounds authentically like the user, but elevated.

### **5.3 Found Poetry and the Erasure Technique**

**Found Poetry** is the literary equivalent of a collage. **Erasure** (or **Blackout**) poetry is a specific subset where the poet takes an existing text and "erases" words to reveal a poem hidden within.27

* **The Distillation Process:** This is a subtractive process. It forces the creator to focus on *selection*.  
* **Application:** Ideally, the Claude Skill could perform a "virtual erasure." The prompt would be: "Act as an Erasure Poet. Read the transcript. You may only use words that appear in the text. Select the most vivid 10% of the words and arrange them to reveal the hidden narrative arc."  
* **Result:** This guarantees 100% fidelity to the user's diction while creating a radically new "poetic" structure. It creates the "epic" feel through the sheer density of the distilled ideas.

**6\. The "MFA Workshop" Simulation: Editing for Quality**

Generating the draft is only half the battle. High-quality poetry is made in revision. In MFA (Master of Fine Arts) programs, poets use specific heuristics to edit and improve their work. We can encode these heuristics into the AI’s "Chain of Thought" to force it to critique and improve its own output before showing it to the user.

### **6.1 The "Kill Your Darlings" Heuristic (Adjective Reduction)**

A primary marker of amateur poetry is the over-reliance on adjectives and adverbs to prop up weak nouns and verbs. MFA workshops relentlessly teach students to "Kill Your Darlings"—to remove unnecessary modifiers.30

* **The "Ly" Ban:** A common exercise is to remove every adverb ending in "-ly."  
* **The AI Fix:** The system prompt should include a revision step: "Scan the draft. Identify all adjectives and adverbs. Remove 50% of them. Ensure that the remaining nouns are concrete and the verbs are active."

### **6.2 The Cliché and Sentimentality Filter**

AI is a "cliché engine" because clichés are statistically probable. "Tears like rain," "soaring heights," and "darkest night" are high-probability completions.

* **Academic Standard:** High-quality poetry avoids cliché and sentimentality (unearned emotion).  
* **Revision Prompt:** "Identify any metaphors or similes that are common figures of speech. Replace them with a novel image derived from the specific context of the transcript."

### **6.3 The "Read Aloud" Cadence Check**

Poetry is an oral art. Writers are taught to read their work aloud to check for "clunky" rhythm or unintended tongue twisters.31

* **Syllabic Variation:** Monotony comes from lines having the exact same syllable count or stress pattern repeatedly (unless it's blank verse).  
* **Revision Prompt:** "Simulate reading the poem aloud. Check for rhythmic variety. Ensure that line lengths vary in a way that matches the 'breath' of the content. If a section is frantic, shorten the lines (staccato). If a section is calm, lengthen the lines (legato)."

**7\. Computational Implementation: Prompt Engineering the "Claude Skill"**

To implement these academic frameworks into a functional "Claude Skill," we must translate literary theory into **Prompt Engineering** strategies. We will use **Persona Prompting**, **Chain of Thought (CoT)**, and **Few-Shot Prompting**.

### **7.1 Persona Prompting: The "Editor" vs. The "Bard"**

Standard "helpful assistant" personas produce "diplomatic" and "safe" poetry.26 We need a persona that embodies the expertise of a contemporary poet.

* **Research Insight:** "Persona prompting" assigns a specific role to the model, which shifts the probability distribution of its output toward the vocabulary and logic of that role.32  
* **The Persona:** "You are an expert Poet and Editor with an MFA in Creative Writing and a background in Qualitative Poetic Inquiry. You specialize in the Objectivist and Imagist schools of poetry (e.g., William Carlos Williams, Denise Levertov). You reject archaic 'high poetic' diction (e.g., 'alas', 'dwell') in favor of concrete, modern imagery. Your goal is to transmute spoken transcripts into high-literary art."

### **7.2 Chain of Thought (CoT): Planning the Poem**

**Chain of Thought** prompting improves performance on complex reasoning tasks by asking the model to "think aloud" before generating the final answer.34 For poetry, this prevents the "rhyme trap" by forcing the model to plan the *content* and *imagery* before it starts worrying about the *sound*.

**The CoT Workflow:**

1. **Analysis Phase (Poetic Inquiry):**  
   * "First, analyze the transcript. Identify the 3 core themes."  
   * "Extract a list of 20 **Concrete Nouns** (physical objects) mentioned in the text."  
   * "Identify the **Tone/Voice** of the speaker (e.g., analytical, urgent, nostalgic)."  
2. **Structural Planning:**  
   * "Select a form based on the content:  
     * If narrative: Use **Loose Blank Verse** or **Modern Epic** structure.  
     * If meditative: Use **Lyrical Free Verse** with organic lineation.  
     * If dense/informational: Use **Prose Poetry**."  
   * "Plan the arc of the poem (Beginning, Middle, End)."  
3. **Drafting Phase (Objective Correlative):**  
   * "Write the poem. **Constraint:** You must use at least 10 of the extracted Concrete Nouns. Use the *Objective Correlative* technique: do not name emotions; describe the objects to evoke the emotions."  
   * "**Negative Constraint:** Do not use the following archaic words: *midst, amongst, whilst, uncurls, tapestry, symphony, gleam, bestow*."  
4. **Revision Phase (MFA Workshop):**  
   * "Critique your own draft. Remove weak adjectives. Check for forced rhymes (if any). Ensure the line breaks follow the breath of the speaker. Rewrite the final version."

### **7.3 Few-Shot Prompting: Showing, Not Just Telling**

**Few-Shot Prompting** involves providing examples of the desired output in the prompt.36 To fix the "weird words" and "rhyme" issues, the user should provide 1-3 examples of the *style* they want (e.g., a snippet of a modern free verse poem or a prose poem).

* **Application:** "Here is an example of the 'Lyrical Prose' style I want: \[Insert example of high-quality modern prose poetry\]. Notice the lack of rhyme and the focus on concrete imagery. Generate the new poem following this stylistic approach."

**8\. Specific Frameworks for "Epic" and "Lyrical" Outputs**

The user has two specific desired outputs: "Epic Poems" and "Lyrical Prose." We can now define the specific parameters for these.

### **8.1 The "Ideation Epic"**

* **Structure:** Stichic (continuous) or divided into numbered Cantos (sections).  
* **Length:** 50+ lines (to capture the "rich information").  
* **Device:** **Anaphora** (repetition of a starting phrase). Instead of rhyme, use Anaphora to create rhythm.  
  * *Example:* "It was the data that saved us / It was the data that drowned us / It was the data we ate...".38  
* **Content:** Must include a **Catalog** (list) of the ideas generated in the session.

### **8.2 The "Lyrical Ideation" (Prose Poem)**

* **Structure:** Paragraph blocks.  
* **Rhythm:** **Parataxis** (short sentences placed side-by-side). "The idea failed. The screen went black. We started over."  
* **Device:** **Internal Rhyme** and **Assonance** (vowel repetition) rather than End-Rhyme. "The *light* and the *white* noise of the room."  
* **Content:** Highly compressed distillation of the transcript.

**9\. Conclusion**

The "weird words" and "loss of structure" the user experiences are not inevitable limitations of AI; they are the default behaviors of a probabilistic model that has not been given a literary framework. By shifting the AI's role from "generating a poem" (which triggers the "Stochastic Parrot" of public domain doggerel) to "performing Poetic Inquiry" (which triggers an analytical, extractive process), we can align the output with the user's high standards.

The key lies in the **Constraint of the Real**: forcing the AI to use the concrete imagery (Objective Correlative) and authentic vocabulary (Verbatim Theatre) found in the transcript, and structuring that raw material using the organic logic of the speaker's breath (Levertov’s Lineation). This transforms the Claude Skill from a generator of "funny" rhymes into an instrument of serious literary transmutation, capable of producing epics and lyrics that honor the depth of the user's ideation.
