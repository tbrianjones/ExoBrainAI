# Title Generation Framework

A comprehensive framework for creating effective titles and headlines across all digital media types. Grounded in research on psychology, SEO, journalism, and copywriting.

## 1. Psychological Foundations

### The Curiosity Gap
Readers experience cognitive discomfort when they perceive a gap between what they know and what they want to know. This activates the brain's reward circuits. Effective titles create genuine curiosity that the content satisfies; pure clickbait that disappoints damages trust.

### Loss Aversion
People feel the pain of loss roughly 2x as strongly as the pleasure of equivalent gains. Titles that identify real problems and position content as solutions tap into this motivator.

### Pattern Disruption
Reworking familiar sayings or challenging conventional wisdom creates "pattern interrupts" that demand attention. The brain releases dopamine when encountering something novel.

### Emotional Processing
Emotional responses precede rational analysis. Titles evoking specific emotions (curiosity, amusement, desire) activate deeper brain structures than rational appeals, creating stronger memory encoding. Initial engagement decisions happen within 50 milliseconds.

## 2. Media Type Specifications

### Blog Posts

**Length**: 50-60 characters; 6-8 words. Longer headlines get truncated in search results.

**What Works**:
- Numbers increase CTR by 36%; odd numbers (3, 5, 7) outperform even numbers
- "How to" headlines tell readers precisely what they'll learn
- Write numbers numerically (5, not "five"); they pop from surrounding text
- Action verbs inspire action
- Power words: "proven," "boost," "essential," "complete"

**Effective Formulas**:
- `[Number] + [Adjective] + [Keyword] + [Promise]`
- `How to [Achieve Result] in [Timeframe]`
- `The Complete Guide to [Topic]`
- `[Number] Ways to [Desired Outcome]`

**Accuracy vs. Catchy**: Educational content prioritizes clarity and SEO. Entertainment content has more latitude for cleverness, but search engines don't understand humor; clever titles may harm discoverability.

---

### News Articles

**Core Principles**:
- Accuracy first; headlines must fairly reflect the story's theme
- Headlines summarize; they don't repeat the lede verbatim
- The headline is more important than anything else, including the lede

**Grammar Conventions (Headlinese)**:
- Present tense for immediacy: "Governor signs bill"
- Infinitive for future: "Governor to sign bill"
- Active voice, subject-verb construction
- Omit articles (a, an, the) and forms of "to be"
- Replace "and" with commas
- No period at end
- Single quotation marks for claims or allegations
- Only capitalize first word and proper nouns

**Breaking News vs. Features**:
- Breaking: prioritize clarity, accuracy, immediacy; state essential facts
- Features: can use creative approaches; question headlines, curiosity gaps, narrative hooks

---

### Email Subject Lines

**Length**: 35-50 characters. Over half of emails open on mobile; longer lines get truncated.

**Marketing Emails**:
- Deep personalization beyond first name
- Segment by industry, job role, behavior
- Authentic urgency (FOMO)
- Strategic emoji use can increase opens 25-50% in some industries
- Avoid spam triggers: "free," "guarantee," "no risk," ALL CAPS, excessive punctuation

**Professional/Business**:
- Reference company name or specific context
- Clarity about purpose
- Tone matches the relationship
- CEOs respond to bottom-line impact; marketers to lead generation

**Personal**:
- Conversational tone
- Specific subject matter
- Context for the relationship

### Social Media

| Platform | Character Limit | Recommended | Notes |
|----------|----------------|-------------|-------|
| YouTube | 100 | 50-60 chars | Title is the most important ranking factor |
| Twitter/X | 280 | Concise | 1-2 hashtags max |
| Instagram | 2,200 | 3-5 hashtags | Captions should reflect how people search |
| TikTok | 2,200 | Keywords in text overlays | Visual recognition reads on-screen text |
| LinkedIn | 3,000 | 3 hashtags | Professional, industry-keyword-focused |

**YouTube Specifics**:
- Front-load keywords for searchability
- Power words: curiosity triggers (Secret, Hidden, Shocking), authority (Expert, Best, Proven), urgency (Now, Fast, Instantly)
- Create synergy between title and thumbnail
- Avoid clickbait that doesn't deliver; damages watch time and reputation
- Benchmark: 4-5% CTR is typical; A/B testing can improve by 10-25%

**Platform Principle**: Social platforms are search engines more than timelines. Success depends on discoverability optimization.

---

### Academic/Technical Writing

**Characteristics**: Descriptive, direct, accurate, appropriate, interesting, concise, precise, unique.

**Length**: Shorter titles are downloaded more often. Titles over 30 words have far fewer citations. Use the fewest words that describe the contents.

**Best Practices**:
- Avoid waste words: "Studies on," "Investigations on"
- Use specific terms rather than general ones
- Include important keywords for indexing; retrieval weightage for "Title" is higher than other fields
- Write the title last, after paper and abstract are complete

**Subtitles**: Common in social sciences; main title describes the problem, subtitle clarifies scope, method, or setting.

**Question Titles**: Well-suited to conference abstracts; can stir curiosity but list hopes rather than outcomes.

---

### Marketing/Advertising

**Key Statistics**:
- 8 in 10 people read headline copy; only 2 in 10 read the rest
- Straightforward headlines outperform creative alternatives 88% of the time

**Copywriting Frameworks**:

**AIDA**: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

**The 4 U's**: Usefulness, Urgency, Uniqueness, Ultra-specificity

**PRESTO**: Pain, Resonate, Educate, Simplify, Testify, Offer

**What Works**:
- Clear and specific; abstract copy loses attention
- Benefits over features
- Customer-centric language ("you" not "we")
- Specific numbers and data points
- Testimonials produce conversions like nothing else

## 3. SEO Considerations

**Title Tag Length**: 50-60 characters (or ~600 pixels). To avoid Google rewrites, aim for 51-55 characters.

**Keyword Placement**: Front-load primary keywords. Search engines prioritize initial words; users start reading there; truncation hides later words.

**Technical Best Practices**:
- Use hyphens as separators (not pipes or brackets)
- Ensure title accurately represents page content
- Match search intent
- Don't keyword stuff

**Google Title Rewrites**: Google rewrites ~60% of title tags. Triggers: keyword stuffing, misleading titles, poor alignment with search intent, inaccurate representation.

## 4. Universal Formulas

| Formula | Example |
|---------|---------|
| How-To | "How to [Achieve Desired Outcome]" |
| Listicle | "[Number] Ways to [Desired Outcome]" |
| Question | "Are You Making These [Topic] Mistakes?" |
| Ultimate Guide | "The Complete Guide to [Topic]" |
| Negative | "Stop Doing [Bad Thing]" |
| Reason Why | "Why [Thing] Matters for [Audience]" |
| Secret/Little-Known | "The Secret to [Desired Outcome]" |
| Newsjacking | "[Trending Topic]: What It Means for [Audience]" |

## 5. Accuracy vs. Engagement: The Trust Trade-off

### Research Findings
- Clickbait significantly reduces credibility
- Sources with low pre-existing credibility suffer most from clickbait
- 63% of UK news audiences believe clickbait reduces trust
- Accurate headlines paired with full articles lead to highest recognition and comprehension

### What Gets Perceived as Clickbait
- Manipulative "Wh" questions (Who, What, When framed deceptively)
- Unusual punctuation
- Negative superlatives ("worst," "never")
- Withholding essential information

### The Balance
Sensational headlines may drive immediate engagement but risk misleading readers and eroding trust. Once trust is broken, it's hard to win back.

**Universal Rule**: Promise only what you can deliver. The most effective headlines create genuine curiosity that the content satisfies.

## 7. Generation Process

### Step 1: Identify Context
- What type of content is this?
- Where will it be published?
- Who is the audience?
- What action do you want readers to take?

### Step 2: Extract Core Message
- What is the single most important takeaway?
- What problem does this solve?
- What benefit does the reader gain?

### Step 3: Apply Constraints
- Character/word limits for the platform
- SEO keywords if applicable
- Brand voice requirements
- Accuracy requirements

### Step 4: Generate Options
- Create 5-10 title variations
- Use different formulas for each
- Vary tone: serious, curious, urgent, playful
- Include at least one straightforward option

### Step 5: Evaluate
- Does it accurately represent the content?
- Will it display fully on the platform?
- Does it create genuine (not false) curiosity?
- Does it match the audience's expectations?
- Would you click on it?

---

## 8. Quality Criteria

### Must Have
- **Accurate**: Reflects actual content; no misleading promises
- **Clear**: Meaning is immediately understood
- **Complete**: Contains enough information to set expectations

### Should Have
- **Engaging**: Creates interest or curiosity
- **Specific**: Uses concrete details over vague generalities
- **Optimized**: Appropriate for platform and search

### Avoid
- False promises or exaggeration
- Vague, generic phrasing
- Keyword stuffing
- Excessive punctuation or caps
- Withholding essential context to manufacture curiosity
